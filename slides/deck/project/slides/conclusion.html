Conclusion

What we learned, and what we would try next

1

Clean the data first: 60 % of the official test set was already in training

2

Gated variants win by a small, real margin and learn faster; CIFG is the best trade-off

3

Learning rate and optimizer matter more than the choice of cell

Next steps

  • Biomedical word vectors and sub-word tokens for drug names
  • Attention pooling instead of the last hidden state
  • Ordinal loss on the 1–10 rating to rescue the neutral class
  • A fine-tuned BioBERT as an upper bound

Code, results and report: github.com/Anyslouzal/lstm-drug-reviews · Thank you — questions? · 11

Three messages to remember. First, data quality comes before modelling: without de-duplication, 60 % of the test set would have been memorised. Second, the non-vanilla cells are better by a small but real margin, significant at equal parameter budget, and they learn faster; CIFG gives the best trade-off between accuracy, size and speed. Third, the learning rate and the optimizer matter more than the cell itself. Next, we would try biomedical word vectors and sub-word tokens, attention pooling over all hidden states, an ordinal loss on the original rating to help the neutral class, and a fine-tuned BioBERT as an upper bound.
