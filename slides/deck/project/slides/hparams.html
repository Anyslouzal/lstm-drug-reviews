Results · hyper-parameters (vanilla baseline)

Learning rate and optimizer matter most

LR: 1e-3 to 3e-3; 3e-3 peaks in half the epochs

Optimizer: Adam ≈ AdamW > RMSprop ≫ SGD

Init: Xavier +0.9 F1, close to seed noise

Batch: 32–64 gain ≈ 1 F1 but cost 2–3× time

One factor changed at a time from the base (Adam, lr 1e-3, batch 128, default init) · 09

We changed one factor at a time on the vanilla baseline. The learning rate is the most sensitive knob: 1e-4 and 3e-4 are far too slow for ten epochs, 1e-2 is unstable, and 1e-3 and 3e-3 tie, with 3e-3 reaching its best score twice as fast. Adaptive optimizers clearly win: Adam and AdamW are equivalent, RMSprop is a bit worse, and SGD with momentum is either too slow or unstable even with gradient clipping. Initialisation changes less than one point: Xavier is best, orthogonal recurrent weights learn faster at the start but end at the same place; these gaps are close to the seed-to-seed noise we measured, about 0.4 points. Smaller batches give more updates per epoch and gain about one point, at two to three times the training time; batch 512 does too few updates in ten epochs. We used these results to build the final model.
